## Why People Look Beyond Angie's List in the First Place

Angie's List — sorry, Angi — still works. It's not dead. But the model has shifted in ways that push small operators to look elsewhere. A few things come up over and over in contractor forums and small business groups:

- **Lead costs add up fast.** You pay per lead whether or not it converts. A $60 lead that ghosts you is just gone.
- **You don't own the relationship.** The customer belongs to the platform. Repeat business has to happen in spite of the platform, not because of it.
- **Ranking is a black box.** You can do everything "right" and still show up behind a competitor who's paying more for placement.
- **The merger concentrated power.** Angi and HomeAdvisor under one roof means less real competition between the two biggest names.

None of this is a reason to panic. It's just a reason to diversify. Most smart operators don't put all their eggs in one marketplace — they spread across a few, and increasingly, they're building their own lead-capture presence so they're not 100% dependent on someone else's algorithm.

## The Big Marketplace Angie's List Competitors

Let's get the obvious ones out of the way first. These are platforms where you list your services and pay for leads. They're not directory builders — they're the directories.

**Thumbtack** is probably the most mentioned alternative. Instead of a flat per-lead fee, you pay per response, usually $8 to $150 depending on the trade, with most landing in the $25–$75 range. There's no annual fee. The "Instant Match" feature auto-assigns you jobs you're qualified for, which some pros love and others find annoying because you can't always vet the customer first.

**HomeAdvisor** is technically an Angie's List competitor, but since they're owned by the same parent company (Angi Inc.), it's more like a sister product. HomeAdvisor skews toward bigger, longer projects — think full renovations rather than a one-off faucet repair. The lead model is similar, and the overlap with Angi is real.

**Houzz** is the niche play for anyone in remodeling, interior design, or the luxury home market. If your work photographs well and you're targeting higher-end clients, Houzz can outperform the generalist platforms. It's also a place where design-forward contractors actually get discovered.

**Yelp** isn't a direct Angie's List clone, but for "near me" local searches it's still a monster. The review system is the whole product. The catch: Yelp's advertising model is aggressive, and the review filter can be unpredictable.

**BuildZoom** connects homeowners with licensed contractors and uses public data to verify licenses and insurance instead of running its own background checks. It's free for homeowners; businesses pay to advertise.

**Porch** is geared toward small and mid-sized service businesses. It offers custom profiles, project highlights, and a "Vetted Pro" upgrade that gives you 5% back on leads the platform generates.

> The pattern across all of these is the same: you pay to play, you don't own the customer, and your visibility depends on the platform's rules. That's fine as one channel. It's risky as your only channel.
